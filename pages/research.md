# Research

Economies emerge out of many millions of transactions. This perspective lets me ask compelling empirical and theoretical research questions, from the physics of financial transactions to the structure and measurement of entire payment systems. Most of my work follows from and contributes to this idea. I have also contributed to various projects across network science and computational social science.

### Real-world walk processes & transaction data
People and companies leave a trail of transactions behind them as they go about their business. This means there is a treasure trove of data about the real economy sitting on the servers of payment processing institutions, worldwide. On the applied side, I develop methods and tools that facilitate the analysis of such datasets. On the theoretical side, currency systems are a compelling example with which to advance a theory of real-world walk processes on networks -- from activity-driven models of financial transactions to methods for measuring money velocity.

### Firm-level production networks
Companies are especially similar to their close competitors, not to their trading partners. This has practical implications for the analysis of production networks and give us precise terms for the local structural features that may be key to understanding their routine function, failure, and growth. It also places production networks into the same ''category'' of network as protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks, where our intutions from social networks can lead us astray.
